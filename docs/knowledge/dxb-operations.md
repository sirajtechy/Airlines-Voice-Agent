# Dubai International (DXB) — ground operations for disrupted passengers

Reference knowledge for RAAHI. Slow-changing operational facts that no live tool
returns, and that a stranded passenger asks about constantly. Everything here is
general guidance, not a live status: route closures, delays and entry rules
always come from the tools, never from this document.

## Terminals and concourses

- **Terminal 3** is Emirates' home. Concourses A, B and C, all connected airside.
- **Concourse A** — A380 gates (A1–A24). Reached by the automated train from B.
- **Concourse B** — the main Terminal 3 building. Check-in, immigration, most
  connections, and the largest transfer desk area.
- **Concourse C** — mainly Boeing 777 gates (C1–C50), walkable from B.
- **Terminal 2** — flydubai and some partners. **Not** connected airside to
  Terminal 3; a transfer requires landside transport and re-clearing security.
- **Terminal 1** — other international carriers, connected to Concourse D.

## Where to go when things go wrong

- **Transfer desks** are in Concourse B and Concourse C, before immigration.
  Go there **before** clearing passport control if your onward flight is affected.
  Clearing immigration to ask at check-in wastes an hour and may need a visa.
- **Emirates ticketing / city check-in** is landside in Terminal 3 departures.
- **Airport passenger-support desks** handle accommodation for very large
  disruptions, and are separate from the airline transfer desk.
- If your onward flight is more than roughly 8 hours away and you hold the right
  documents, the transfer desk may issue a hotel voucher; airside hotels
  (Terminal 3 Hotel, sleep pods in Concourse A and B) are the alternative.

## Connection times

- **Minimum connection time at DXB is 75 minutes** for Emirates-to-Emirates
  within Terminal 3, and longer if the itinerary changes terminal.
- A connection under 75 minutes booked on one ticket is still Emirates'
  responsibility to re-accommodate if the inbound is late.
- **Terminal 3 to Terminal 2 needs about 3 hours** — it is landside, and a
  UAE visa or visa-on-arrival eligibility is required to make it.
- Missing a connection on a **single ticket** means Emirates rebooks you at no
  charge. On **separate tickets**, the second carrier is not obliged to, which
  is the single most expensive misunderstanding a transit passenger makes.

## Documents in transit

- Passengers transiting airside within Terminal 3 do **not** normally need a UAE
  visa. Leaving the airport does.
- Entry restrictions can apply to transit passengers even when they never leave
  the airside area — the transit rules tool is authoritative on this, not this
  document.
- Onward travel is checked at the **origin** airport. Being allowed to board in
  Kampala does not mean being allowed to enter or transit Dubai.

## Baggage

- On a single ticket, checked bags are normally through-tagged to the final
  destination and do not need collecting in Dubai.
- On separate tickets, bags must be reclaimed and re-checked, which requires
  clearing immigration.
- **Delayed baggage** must be reported at the arrivals baggage desk before
  leaving the airport; keep the Property Irregularity Report (PIR) reference.
- Emirates' standard checked allowance is by weight in Economy (typically 20–35kg
  by fare), and by piece on routes to and from the Americas. Cabin baggage is
  normally 7kg in Economy and two pieces totalling 14kg in Business and First.

## Terms a caller may use

- **IROPS** — irregular operations: delays, cancellations, diversions.
- **Misconnect** — missing an onward flight because the inbound arrived late.
- **Protection / re-accommodation** — being moved to another flight at no cost.
- **Involuntary rerouting** — the airline moves you; different rights from a
  voluntary change.
- **PIR** — Property Irregularity Report, the delayed-baggage reference.
- **Turnaround** — the ground time between an aircraft arriving and departing.
