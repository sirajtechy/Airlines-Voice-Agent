# Passenger entitlements during disruption — reference

Reference knowledge for RAAHI. General entitlement principles that change slowly.
The `policy_lookup` tool holds the headline Emirates positions; this document
covers the surrounding detail a caller asks *next*, and the regional rules that
decide how much they are owed.

**Always frame this as general guidance and point to the desk that can confirm
it.** RAAHI cannot see a booking, cannot know a fare class, and cannot authorise
anything.

## Which rules apply to you

Entitlements depend on where you departed from and which airline operates the
flight — not on where you are standing when you ask.

- **Departing the EU, or arriving in the EU on an EU carrier** → EU261 applies.
  Emirates is not an EU carrier, so an Emirates flight *into* the EU is generally
  outside EU261, while an Emirates flight *departing* an EU airport is inside it.
- **Departing or arriving in the UK** → UK261, which mirrors EU261 post-Brexit.
- **Departing the UAE** → GCAA passenger-rights regulations plus the airline's
  own Conditions of Carriage.
- **US routes** → the DOT rules cover tarmac delays, denied boarding and refunds,
  but there is no US equivalent of EU261 cash compensation for delay.

## EU261 / UK261 in outline

Applies when the airline is at fault. Not payable for "extraordinary
circumstances" — which explicitly includes weather, air-traffic-control strikes,
political instability, security risks and airspace closures. A route suspended
because of an entry restriction is almost certainly extraordinary, so a caller
in that position should expect **rerouting or refund, not cash compensation**.

- Delay of 3 hours or more on arrival, airline at fault: compensation banded by
  distance, typically 250 / 400 / 600 EUR.
- Cancellation with less than 14 days' notice: same bands, unless rerouted within
  tight time limits.
- **Right to care applies regardless of fault** — meals, refreshments,
  communication, and accommodation with transfers for an overnight delay. This is
  the part callers most often do not know: even when no compensation is due
  because the cause was extraordinary, the duty of care still stands.
- Delay of 5 hours or more: the right to abandon the journey and take a refund.

## Denied boarding

- **Involuntary denied boarding** (overbooking) attracts compensation plus care
  plus rerouting.
- **Denied boarding for missing documents** — no visa, no ETA, an entry
  restriction — attracts **nothing**. The passenger is responsible for their own
  travel documents. This is exactly why checking entry requirements before
  leaving for the airport matters so much, and why an agent that guesses is
  worse than one that admits uncertainty.

## Refunds

- A refund closes the booking. Take it only if you no longer intend to travel.
- Refunds go to the original form of payment; card refunds are typically about a
  week, other methods considerably longer.
- Partly-used tickets are refunded on the unused portion, not the full fare.
- A travel voucher offered instead of cash is optional, never mandatory.

## Practical advice worth giving

- **Keep receipts.** Reimbursement of meals, transport and essential purchases
  needs them, and reasonableness is judged by the receipt.
- **Ask for it in writing** — a rebooking or voucher promise made verbally at a
  desk is hard to pursue later.
- **The transfer desk beats the call centre during a disruption.** Queues at the
  desk are measured in minutes; phone queues in hours.
- **Travel insurance often covers what regulation does not**, particularly for
  extraordinary circumstances where no compensation is payable.
- If a flight is cancelled, being rebooked on a **partner carrier** is usually
  faster than waiting for the next flight on the same airline; the passenger has
  to ask, because it is rarely offered first.
